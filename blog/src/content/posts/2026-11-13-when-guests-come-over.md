If your cat spends the first half of a visit in another room, let the visit happen around that choice. You can make an evening easier by arranging a quiet place before the doorbell rings and giving everyone a simple way to respond if the cat comes out.

## Get the room ready before the first knock

Think about what usually changes when people arrive. Coats pile up in the hall, chairs move and doors open more often. Check that your cat's usual route to a quiet room will still be clear when the hallway is full. If bags or shoes tend to collect by the entrance, give guests another place to put them.

[Cats Protection's visitor guidance](https://www.cats.org.uk/help-and-advice/caring-for-your-cat/cats-and-your-family/meeting-other-people) suggests letting the cat initiate contact and keeping an escape route available. Give your cat a retreat it already knows and can reach on its own. If you offer a new hiding place, set it out before the visit and keep the familiar one available too. [Cats Protection's hiding guide](https://www.cats.org.uk/help-and-advice/cat-behaviour/cats-hiding) offers examples, including a simple box the cat can leave easily.

## Tell guests what to do in one sentence

Try saying, “If she comes in, stay seated and give her a little room to decide.” That works at the door, before anyone has had time to follow the cat down the hall or gather around the sofa. Ask children and adults to leave the hiding spot alone, even if the cat peeks out.

When the cat approaches, keep the path back clear. A brief look or sniff can be the whole interaction. If the cat turns around, let it walk away. Guests can enjoy seeing the cat from their seats without reaching across the space or making a fuss.

## Keep one part of the evening familiar

Decide who will handle the usual feeding routine while everyone is distracted. Check that the person knows where the food, clean dish and supplies are, and that the cat can reach its usual feeding spot without crossing the busiest part of the room. Avoid using the meal to draw the cat toward a group of people.

## Put things back when the visit ends

Clear the entrance, return chairs to their usual places and reopen any interior routes you temporarily closed. Let your cat come out at its own pace as the house gets quieter. You can note what worked while the evening is fresh. Perhaps the quiet room stayed accessible until someone left a bag at the door. Next time, put the bag somewhere else before your guests arrive.
