A feeding schedule can look complete while a basic setup question remains unanswered. Does the food shown for a tray position match what you put in that physical compartment?

For the Robotail F20, it is worth resolving that question while you are beside the device. The official instructions link the numbered plate positions to app entries. Checking that relationship gives you a clearer basis for loading food and reviewing a schedule.

## Begin with the instructions for your model

Use the [F20 user-guide page](https://robotailpets.com/pages/f20-user-guide) to find the manufacturer's instructions. In the [official PDF manual](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0707/2441/9802/files/f20_521.pdf?v=1785140856), English printed page 06 shows plates 1, 2, 3 and 4 corresponding to positions 1, 2, 3 and 4 in the app. It also directs users to match food type and the order of uploaded pictures to the actual food.

Keep that diagram open while you inspect your setup. If your supplied manual or app looks different, identify the version and ask support to clarify the correspondence before carrying assumptions across.

## Compare one position at a time

Work through the physical marking, the food placed there and the app entry together. Our suggested check is to name each position aloud and point to the corresponding entry as you review it.

This is particularly useful when two people share preparation. Saying “the compartment nearest me” depends on where the person stands. Referring to the marked number gives both people something stable to check against the manual.

If the tray needs alignment, use the model's documented controls. The function-button section describes an alignment operation. Follow that section rather than trying to work out the tray's movement from a marketing photograph.

## Make pictures understandable to the next person

An uploaded food picture helps only when the entry is current and recognisable. When changing what you load, review the associated food entry and image together. Avoid leaving an older image in place because its colour looks similar at thumbnail size.

The manual describes matching pictures and food positions. Our editorial recommendation is to review the match after a change instead of assuming the previous setup still describes the tray. This makes the handover easier for anyone else who uses the feeder.

Keep the record factual. A food image identifies what you intended to place in a position. Your own check establishes what you actually loaded.

## Keep loading and schedule checks distinct

Once the positions make sense, review the intended feeding times separately. A matching picture does not tell you whether you selected the intended time, and a carefully selected time does not confirm the compartment contents.

A short final check can therefore cover two questions. Can you explain which entry belongs to each marked position? Do the selected times match the household's current feeding plan?

If you find an inconsistency, correct the setup using the manual before depending on it. There is no need to invent a new routine simply to make the screen look finished.

## Recheck when the setup changes

The [Robotail F20 manufacturer page](https://robotailpets.com/products/f20) and the [F20 Amazon listing](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0FRZZ12QV) are product-information starting points. For operation, return to the manual for the unit you own.

Make a repeat check part of a food change or a handover to another person. The useful outcome is straightforward. Somebody standing beside the feeder should be able to compare its marked positions with the app and understand what is planned without guessing from the order of a photograph.
